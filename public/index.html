A LITTLE CLARITY

Big questions.
A thoughtful start.

You don’t need a diagnosis to start asking questions about your child’s development.

Where do I start if I’m worried about my child?

You can speak to your GP, health visitor or your child’s school or nursery about your concerns. Write down examples of what you’ve noticed, when it happens and what helps. Include the things your child enjoys and does well.

NHS advice on seeking an assessment ↗
Can a child have both ADHD and autism?

Yes. ADHD and autism can occur together. A clinician considers a child’s full developmental picture and whether further assessment is appropriate, rather than relying on one behaviour or an online checklist.

Learn more from the NHS ↗
What might an assessment involve?

It may include a conversation about your child’s early development and medical history, observations or activities with your child, and information from people who know them well. The format varies by service and assessment. Ask about timing, who will be involved, any adjustments needed and how findings will be shared.

Can support begin before a diagnosis?

You can talk with your child’s school or nursery about the support they need now. In England, a school’s special educational needs co-ordinator (SENCO) is a useful starting point. Your GP or local services can advise on other support; arrangements differ across the UK.
